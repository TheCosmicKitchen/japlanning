Read Japan in an afternoon

You don't need to speak Japanese. Learn katakana and you can sound out menus, shop signs and half the words on a train platform. Add 20 kanji and you can find the exit, the right bath and the right platform.

Step 1 · 一

Katakana カタカナ

Japanese uses katakana for words borrowed from other languages, and a lot of those come from English. コーヒー is "kō-hī", coffee. Once you can sound it out, you can often guess the word. There are 46 basic characters. Tap one to hear it.

ー stretches the vowelビール = bī-ru (beer), not biru (building)
Small ッ doubles the next soundチケット = chi-ket-to (ticket)
Small ャ ュ ョ combineメニュー = me-nyū (menu)
゛ and ゜ change the soundカ ka → ガ ga · ハ ha → バ ba → パ pa

Watch out for look-alikes

シshistrokes go up from the left, like a smile
ツtsustrokes come down from the top
ソsolong stroke drops from the top
ンnlong stroke rises from the bottom

Also mixed up a lot: ク ku vs ケ ke vs タ ta, and ウ u vs ワ wa vs フ fu.

Step 4 · 四

20 must-know kanji 漢字

You'll see these every day: exits, platforms, baths, prices, opening hours. Each card shows the word you'll actually see on signs, and 📷 photos show real signs in Japan. Tap a photo to zoom in.

Onsen tip: the men's bath often has a blue curtain and the women's a red one, but some inns swap the curtains each day so both groups get to use each bath. Read the kanji: 男 men, 女 women.
